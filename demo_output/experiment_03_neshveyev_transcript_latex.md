## TRANSCRIPT (What Speaker SAYS)

### [00:00 - 02:00] Defining Subproduct Systems
> "A short way of defining them, and these are so-called standard subproduct systems, is that it is a collection of finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, indexed by non-negative integers, with the property that the zero space corresponds is one-dimensional, and the space with index $n+m$ is a subspace of the tensor product of the space with index $n$ and space with index $m$. So there is another useful way of thinking about such subproduct systems... there is a one-to-one correspondence between such subproduct systems and homogeneous ideals in the algebra of non-commutative polynomials in $N$ variables."

### [02:00 - 04:00] Associated Operator Algebras
> "If you have such a system, you can define certain algebras very similar to how you define Cuntz algebras... Let me denote by $F_n$ the projection from the $n$-th tensor power... onto the subspace $H_n$. Then we consider the Fock space associated with the subproduct system, which is the direct sum simply of all these fibers... For every vector in the first space, you have a creation operator..."

### [06:00 - 09:00] Arveson's Symmetric Systems
> "One example, an important example... is so-called Arveson's symmetric subproduct system. In this case, we consider the ideal generated by all commutators. So in other words, the quotient is just the usual algebra of polynomials... Arveson concluded that the corresponding Cuntz-Pimsner algebra is $C(S^{2N-1})$."

### [14:04 - 16:00] Cartan Subproduct Systems
> "There is a broad class of examples which covers these Arveson examples... We fix once and for all a simply connected semisimple compact Lie group. Recall that irreducible representations... are parameterized by dominant integral weights... This component is called the Cartan component of the tensor product."

### [24:48 - 26:00] Main Theorem
> "Assume that $q > 1$ and we have this conjecture... then the Cuntz-Pimsner algebra defined by this subproduct system is isomorphic to $C(G^\lambda \backslash G_q / G^\lambda)$. Here $G^\lambda$ is the stabilizer..."

## DEFINITIONS & THEOREMS

**Definition (Standard Subproduct System):** A collection $H = (H_n)_{n \ge 0}$ such that $H_0 = \mathbb{C}$ and $H_{n+m} \subseteq H_n \otimes H_m$ isometrically. 1-to-1 correspondence with homogeneous ideals $I \subset T(\mathbb{C}^N)$.

**Arveson-Douglas Conjecture:** If ideal $I$ contains all commutators $[X_i, X_j]$, then $\mathcal{O}_H$ is commutative.

**Main Theorem:** For $q > 1$ and assuming the asymptotic orthogonality conjecture, $\mathcal{O}_{(V_{n\lambda})} \cong C(G^\lambda \backslash G_q / G^\lambda)$.

## SUMMARY

The lecture discusses C*-algebras associated with subproduct systems, which generalize Cuntz algebras and correspond to homogeneous ideals in non-commutative polynomial rings. The speaker introduces "Cartan subproduct systems," constructed from the highest weight components (Cartan components) of tensor powers of irreducible representations of compact Lie groups (and their quantum deformations).

A key technical focus is the asymptotic behavior of projections onto these Cartan components as the weights scale. Under the conjecture (proven for $SU(N)$), the main theorem establishes that the Cuntz-Pimsner algebra of a Cartan subproduct system is isomorphic to the algebra of continuous functions on a specific quantum homogeneous space.
